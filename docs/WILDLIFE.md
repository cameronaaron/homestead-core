# When wildlife turns up

Raccoon kits, a sick possum: people bring us wild animals because we're
"the farm". We are not a wildlife rehabilitator and can't legally become
one casually, so our job is to get the animal to someone who is.

## The rule

In California, it is unlawful to possess live wildlife except as the law
allows. Under the rules effective August 13, 2025, an unpermitted person may
temporarily confine an injured, diseased or orphaned animal for transfer to a
permitted rehabilitator or CDFW for **no more than 24 hours** (a California
licensed veterinarian gets 48). Within that same 24 hours, the finder must
give the rehabilitator or CDFW their name and phone number, the species and
its condition, when and where it was found, and where it is confined. Contact
CDFW at RehabWildlife@wildlife.ca.gov or (916) 358-2790
([14 CCR § 679.2](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Laboratories/Wildlife-Health/Rehab/Regulations)).
Raising raccoons ourselves to release "in the back" isn't an option.

## What to do

1. Keep people, kids, dogs and our own animals away. CDFW says not to capture,
   handle, confine, feed or water wildlife without guidance from a trained
   professional. Raccoons can carry rabies and roundworm.
2. Call a rehabilitator from the list below **before** moving or driving the
   animal anywhere. Most don't pick up. They'll tell you whether to bring it
   in, and some babies are better left for the mother to return.
3. If a professional tells you to contain it, wear gloves and use a ventilated
   box somewhere dark and quiet, away from people and animals.
4. Post in Discord #daily-log what came in and where it went.

## Domestic animals are different

Stray or community **cats** are Kiki's Cat Club's work (TNR through the
county program, fostering, adoption). Dogs and other pets go to LA Animal
Services.
